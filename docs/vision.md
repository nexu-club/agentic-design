# Open Design — Vision

## The one-liner

**Open Design is the agentic design workspace — a chat-native canvas where humans and AI agents co-create products, from PRD to pixel to production.**

## The thesis

Every product-building tool stack today is shaped for humans working in sequence:
- PM writes PRD in Notion
- Designer draws in Figma
- Engineer builds in VS Code
- QA tests in Jira
- Growth measures in Mixpanel

Each tool optimizes its local step. None of them understand the **product** as a living artifact.

**Agents don't work that way.** Agents can hold the whole context — the spec, the design, the code, the telemetry — in one place. They should.

**Open Design is the workspace shaped for humans + agents, not humans alone.**

## Why design first

We could have started anywhere in the product pipeline. We start at **design** because:

1. **Design is where context becomes concrete** — a pixel decision forces a PM decision and an engineering decision simultaneously
2. **Design tools are visual** — easier to make multi-agent collaboration legible than in pure text
3. **Figma is a single point of failure** in most teams today — one tool everyone depends on, no agent-native alternative
4. **AI models are getting good at visual generation fast** — the timing window for an agentic design tool is now

## What makes it different from "Figma + AI plugin"

| Figma + AI plugin | Open Design |
|---|---|
| AI is a feature | Agents are teammates |
| One human, one canvas | Multi-agent + multi-human in one room |
| Designs are files | Designs are artifacts inside a living chat |
| Hand-off to engineering | Engineering agents already in the room |
| "Generate and edit" | "Coordinate a team" |

## What "Agentic" means here

An **agentic** design workspace means:
- Agents are **first-class participants** with their own identities, permissions, and memory
- You **@mention** agents like you'd @mention colleagues
- Agents can **kick off tasks for other agents** without a human in the loop
- The workspace **accumulates decisions** — agents reference past decisions to stay consistent
- Humans can **observe, intervene, or take over** at any time

## Non-goals

- We are not building "AI Figma" (that's just adding features to an old shape)
- We are not building a chatbot that produces designs (that's text-first; we're visual-first)
- We are not targeting enterprise design system management (yet)
- We are not trying to replace human designers (we want them to have a team of agents)

## 24-month bet

If we're right, in 24 months:
- Building a product starts in Open Design, not in Figma or Linear or Notion
- Small teams (2–20) can ship at the quality bar of teams 5× their size
- A "one-person company" with 10 agents is a real, commonly-encountered team shape
- Design files are obsolete — the living chat **is** the design

If we're wrong, we'll have built a really nice agent-native design tool anyway.
